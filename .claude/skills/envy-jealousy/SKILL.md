# 嫉妒与妒忌 (Envy & Jealousy)

## Overview

Envy and jealousy are universal human emotions that arise when seeing others obtain what we desire, creating feelings of injustice and resentment. Evolutionary psychology suggests this originates from the instinct for relative status competition. Charlie Munger noted that envy is the most unenjoyable yet most common sin. It brings no direct benefit to oneself, only painful comparison, but evolutionarily may have motivated us to catch up with competitors. Envy often drives irrational behaviors, such as doing things that harm others and benefit oneself just to prevent others from doing well. Understanding this tendency can help us manage personal emotions with awareness, and not let jealousy cause us to make mistakes. In organizations, also guard against internal jealousy triggered by unfair incentives that undermine team cooperation.

## When to Apply

- Management: Salary systems should be as fair and transparent as possible to avoid triggering employee jealousy; when distributing tasks and honors, consider team psychology
- Investment: Overcome the impulse to be envious when seeing others make quick money, and stick to rational strategies
- Personal development: Cultivate contentment and appreciate others' success, combating envy, and avoid emotional decision-making due to jealousy
- Comparison awareness: Realize that humans are naturally inclined to compare, but "comparison with self" is healthier than "comparison with others"

## How to Use

1. Recognize when envy is driving your emotions or decisions
2. Focus on your own progress rather than comparing with others
3. Practice appreciating others' success genuinely
4. Design systems that reduce unnecessary comparison triggers
5. Cultivate contentment as an antidote to envy

## Examples

### Example 1: Colleague Promotion
When a colleague gets promoted, some might spread rumors to discredit the person due to envy, even though this brings no benefit to themselves.

### Example 2: Stock Market Envy
Stock market investors seeing others get rich easily may become envious, then follow trends to take risks, often resulting in buying high and being caught in losses.

### Example 3: Trojan War
Historically, the "Trojan War" was mythologized as caused by jealousy among goddesses, also reflecting the destructive power of envy emotions.

## Core Principles

- Envy is the most unenjoyable yet common sin
- Envy drives irrational behavior that harms both self and others
- Humans are naturally inclined to compare, but inward comparison is healthier
- Systems should reduce unnecessary comparison triggers
- Contentment and appreciation for others' success are antidotes to envy

## Warning Signs / Common Mistakes / Benefits

- Warning: Jealousy can cause people to damage others without benefiting themselves
- Warning: Internal competition for unfair rewards can destroy team cooperation
- Mistake: Making decisions based on what others have rather than your own needs
- Mistake: Spreading negativity about successful people out of envy
- Benefit: Understanding envy helps manage emotional reactions
- Benefit: Focusing on self-comparison leads to greater personal satisfaction
