# 权威影响 (Authority Bias)

## Overview

Authority Bias refers to the tendency for people to obey authority directives, even when it goes against their own judgment or moral standards. Milgram's famous shock experiment demonstrated that ordinary people can perform cruel acts under authority commands. Authority bias shows that titles and status have enormous psychological influence on people. Obeying authority is beneficial for group coordination in evolution, but can also be exploited by bad authorities. When discussing this tendency, Munger emphasized being alert to unreasonable obedience and opposing personality worship in companies. Managers must bear responsibility because subordinates will overly depend on their instructions; they should make prudent decisions and encourage subordinates to think independently.

## When to Apply

- Organization: Establish open culture, allowing subordinates to raise objections to leader suggestions, reducing blind obedience risks
- Society: In citizen education, emphasize personal conscience and critical thinking, not blindly believing authority figures
- Self: When facing "expert" or "boss" opinions, retain independent judgment, verify or seek second opinions when necessary

## How to Use

1. Question authority directives that don't make sense
2. Create cultures where dissent is welcomed
3. Separate the person from the validity of their arguments
4. Seek multiple perspectives before accepting authority views
5. Recognize that authority can be wrong

## Examples

### Example 1: Leadership Mistakes
In enterprises, if leaders make wrong decisions, subordinates rarely publicly oppose, most will obey and execute—this is authority effect.

### Example 2: Milgram Experiment
During WWII, Nazi soldiers often massacred civilians with "obeying orders" as excuse. Milgram's experiment proved that ordinary people, under researchers' (authority) demands, continued increasing shock intensity to others; up to 65% of subjects obeyed to the end.

### Example 3: Medical Settings
In medical settings, nurses often follow doctors' medication instructions, even if doses are abnormal, possibly not questioning.

## Core Principles

- Titles and status have enormous psychological influence
- Ordinary people can commit wrongs under authority pressure
- Evolutionarily, authority obedience benefits coordination
- Bad authorities can exploit this tendency
- Leaders should encourage independent thinking

## Warning Signs / Common Mistakes / Benefits

- Warning: Blind obedience to authority causes errors and harm
- Warning: "Just following orders" is not a moral defense
- Mistake: Assuming authority figures are always correct
- Mistake: Not questioning authority even when something seems wrong
- Benefit: Clear authority can improve group coordination
- Benefit: Independent thinking culture reduces errors
