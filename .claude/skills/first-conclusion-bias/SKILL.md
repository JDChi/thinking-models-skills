# 首因效应 (First-Conclusion Bias)

## Overview

First-Conclusion Bias refers to Munger's analogy that the human brain is like an egg: "The first sperm (idea) that enters causes the egg to close." That is, we tend to stick to first impressions or conclusions. Once a preliminary view is formed in the mind, when receiving other information, we tend to support the original conclusion and ignore or reject contrary evidence. This bias explains why first impressions have great influence and how confirmation bias forms (the tendency to search for information supporting one's own view). It makes us reflect that the decision-making process should avoid hastily drawing conclusions, because once a conclusion is reached, it's hard to remain objective. Munger suggests using checklists and multidisciplinary perspectives to delay judgment and reduce first-conclusion effect. Also, this model tells us that mindsets once formed are hard to change, so when educating children or training employees, what views are taught initially matters a lot.

## When to Apply

- Decision-making: Deliberately delay final conclusions, see and think more, don't let the first idea casually take over
- Meeting discussions: Avoid having leaders or authorities express opinions first, to prevent everyone blindly following the first view; can first anonymously collect ideas, then discuss
- Marketing: Creating the first perception of a product is crucial; brand positioning and user initial experience deeply affect subsequent user loyalty, so new product launches should start with high quality

## How to Use

1. Deliberately delay forming conclusions until you have full information
2. Seek contrary evidence actively before deciding
3. Use checklists to ensure systematic rather than intuitive evaluation
4. Be aware that first impressions disproportionately influence judgment
5. Create processes that prevent premature closure

## Examples

### Example 1: Job Interviews
In interviews, the candidate's first impression on the interviewer often dominates the entire evaluation (primacy effect); later mediocre performance is easily covered by the original good impression.

### Example 2: Scientific Theories
In scientific research, a generation of authoritative theories may be outdated, but because the academic community mostly learned their theories growing up, they often resist new evidence (unwilling to overturn first impressions).

### Example 3: Investor Bias
If investors first decide a company is excellent, even when bad news emerges later, they tend to rationally explain and continue to be optimistic.

## Core Principles

- "First impressions are powerful" - first impressions dominate judgment
- The brain tends to "close" after first conclusions
- Mindsets once formed are hard to change
- Deliberate delay reduces first-conclusion bias
- What is taught first has lasting influence

## Warning Signs / Common Mistakes / Benefits

- Warning: First impressions can lock in incorrect judgments
- Warning: Confirmation bias often follows first-conclusion bias
- Mistake: Making decisions with incomplete information
- Mistake: Not seeking contrary evidence
- Benefit: First impressions can be leveraged ethically in marketing
- Benefit: Awareness helps prevent premature closure
