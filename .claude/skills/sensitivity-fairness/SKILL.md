# 公平敏感 (Sensitivity to Fairness)

## Overview

Sensitivity to Fairness refers to how humans highly value fairness, and if perceiving injustice, will trigger strong emotional reactions including anger, non-cooperation, or even revenge. This tendency is deeply rooted in human nature; people are willing to sacrifice some benefit for fairness. Fairness tendency explains why people care not only about absolute gains but also about relative fairness (related to relative satisfaction tendency). Social contracts and morality are built on the pursuit of fairness. However, the concept of "fairness" varies with culture and situation, and can be easily manipulated to incite emotions. Managers need to recognize employees' sensitivity to treatment fairness, ensuring systems are transparent and fair to maintain morale. Decision-makers must also balance benefit distribution, pursuing efficiency while considering fairness, otherwise inciting public resentment that harms long-term interests.

## When to Apply

- System design: Establish fair and transparent evaluation and reward systems, ensuring fairness in opportunities and treatment, so employees are more convinced and work harder
- Negotiation: Take care of the other party's fairness perception; extreme exploitation is less conducive to reaching agreements and maintaining relationships; win-win results are more stable
- Policy: Formulating economic policies needs to narrow excessive wealth gaps and ensure basic fairness perception, otherwise long-term will cause social problems

## How to Use

1. Create transparent and consistent evaluation criteria
2. Ensure explanations for decisions are provided
3. Address perceived inequities before they cause resentment
4. Design win-win rather than zero-sum solutions
5. Recognize cultural differences in fairness perceptions

## Examples

### Example 1: Ultimatum Game
In the psychological "ultimatum game," one party proposes how to split money and the other can accept or reject. Experiments find that if the proposal is seriously unfair (one party takes 90%, other gets 10%), subjects often reject even though rejection means neither gets money. This shows people would rather self-harm than accept obvious injustice.

### Example 2: Workplace Promotion Perception
In the workplace, if employees feel promotions depend purely on connections rather than ability, work enthusiasm is severely impacted.

### Example 3: Tax Policy Perception
In society, if taxes are too biased toward the wealthy, it triggers common people to demand "robbing the rich to help the poor"; for social stability, excessive unfairness must be corrected.

## Core Principles

- Humans have deep sensitivity to fairness
- Fairness affects cooperation and emotional reactions
- People will sacrifice personal gain for fairness
- "Fairness" varies by culture and situation
- Perceived fairness impacts morale and compliance

## Warning Signs / Common Mistakes / Benefits

- Warning: Unfair systems destroy morale and cooperation
- Warning: Fairness perceptions can be manipulated for political ends
- Mistake: Assuming one fairness standard applies universally
- Mistake: Ignoring perceived fairness even when actual fairness exists
- Benefit: Fair systems build trust and cooperation
- Benefit: Win-win negotiations are more sustainable
