# 基本归因错误 (Fundamental Attribution Error)

## Overview

Fundamental Attribution Error refers to the tendency for people to attribute others' behaviors to their character qualities while underestimating situational factors. For themselves, they attribute more to environment. This is a common bias in social psychology. Fundamental attribution error causes us to be harsh on others and lenient on ourselves. Not understanding others' situations, we make rash judgments, saying others' success is purely due to ability/failure due to incompetence, ignoring opportunity or difficulty backgrounds. Munger reminds us to "walk in the other's shoes" and consider situational factors. Correcting this bias can give us more empathy and also more accurate judgment of problem root causes (many problems may be due to situational pressure, not personal character defects). In management, if an employee performs poorly, first consider work system problems rather than immediately labeling them lazy.

## When to Apply

- Management: When evaluating employee problems, ask "what environmental/system factors caused this?" rather than blaming employee ability or attitude first
- Interpersonal: When someone's behavior doesn't meet expectations, first consider their circumstances at the time, don't rush to moral judgment
- Self-reflection: Realize you are also often influenced by environment, so don't always attribute success to yourself and failure to luck; view relatively fairly

## How to Use

1. Consider situational factors before making character judgments
2. Ask "what circumstances might explain this behavior?"
3. Apply the same standards to yourself and others
4. Separate the person from the specific behavior
5. Seek more information before judging

## Examples

### Example 1: Red Light Crossing
Seeing a passerby cross against the red light, we easily criticize their character, but they might be rushing to the hospital (situational necessity).

### Example 2: Project Failure Attribution
When a company project fails, outsiders might blame the project manager as incompetent, but sudden market changes or insufficient resources might be the main cause.

### Example 3: Lateness Attribution
When yourself late, you say due to traffic (situation); when others late, you say they are punctual (character).

## Core Principles

- We judge others by character, ourselves by situation
- Fundamental attribution error causes misunderstanding and conflict
- "Walk in the other's shoes" improves understanding
- Many problems are situational, not character-based
- Empathy requires considering circumstances

## Warning Signs / Common Mistakes / Benefits

- Warning: Fundamental attribution error leads to unfair judgments
- Warning: Harsh judgment of others while being lenient on self
- Mistake: Attributing behavior to character without understanding situation
- Mistake: Ignoring how environment shapes behavior
- Benefit: Considering situation leads to more accurate analysis
- Benefit: Empathy improves relationships and reduces conflict
