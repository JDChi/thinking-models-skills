# 事后偏⻅ (Hindsight Bias)

## Overview

Hindsight Bias refers to the tendency for people to believe after an event occurs that they had already known or predicted the outcome, when they had not been that certain at the time. This is commonly known as the "hindsight effect" or "I knew it all along." Hindsight bias makes us overestimate our past judgment ability and become overconfident about the future. Munger believes this prevents us from learning from mistakes, because afterward everyone says "I knew it would be like this," thus missing opportunities to reflect on mistakes. One method to overcome this bias is to write down predictions before decisions, then compare afterward to objectively see the gap. It also inspires us to develop recording habits (like investment journals, decision journals) so the brain doesn't beautify past views afterward.

## When to Apply

- Learning and review: Make expectation lists before projects/investments; after results come out, strictly compare to improve sensitivity to recognizing errors, not letting the brain self-justify afterward
- Risk communication: When people tend to think a risk "should have been known," they may become careless; should emphasize the difficulty of prediction at the time, be vigilant about similar future risks
- Legal and audit: Evaluate behavior considering information at the time; don't judge from hindsight perspective (for example, judicial system rejects "results-oriented theory" to avoid blaming people for not knowing after each incident)

## How to Use

1. Write down predictions before events occur
2. Review predictions against actual outcomes objectively
3. Accept that uncertainty was real at the time of decision
4. Keep journals to prevent memory distortion
5. Recognize others' hindsight bias to maintain humility

## Examples

### Example 1: Stock Market Crash
After a stock market crash, many recall that signs were obvious and they had some expectation, but in fact before the crash they had no clear action to avoid risk.

### Example 2: Exam Results
After exam results are announced, students easily feel "I already knew those questions, just made a momentary oversight," which is also hindsight wisdom as self-comfort.

### Example 3: 9/11 Analysis
After 9/11, various analyses emerged, piecing previously ignored clues into seemingly obvious warnings, as if the tragedy could have been prevented, but in reality no one truly foresaw it.

## Core Principles

- "I knew it all along" is usually false memory
- Hindsight bias prevents learning from mistakes
- Writing predictions before events enables objective review
- Recording habits prevent memory distortion
- Recognizing this bias promotes humility about predictions

## Warning Signs / Common Mistakes / Benefits

- Warning: Hindsight bias leads to overconfidence in future predictions
- Warning: Blaming decision-makers based on outcomes rather than information available
- Mistake: Believing you would have acted differently knowing what you know now
- Mistake: Not recording decisions and predictions for later review
- Benefit: Prediction journaling improves decision quality
- Benefit: Humility about predictions leads to better risk assessment
