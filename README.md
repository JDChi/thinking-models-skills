# Thinking Models Skills

Transforming multidisciplinary mental models into AI-compatible Skills, inspired by Charlie Munger's latticework philosophy.

## Overview

Charlie Munger, Vice Chairman of Berkshire Hathaway, advocates for a multidisciplinary approach to decision-making through "Mental Models." He emphasizes that by mastering core concepts from various disciplines, one can make better decisions in all aspects of life. While Munger did not publish an explicit list of 100 models, his philosophy has inspired many practitioners to compile and systematize these frameworks from multiple domains including mathematics, physics, biology, psychology, and economics.

This project implements these foundational thinking frameworks as standalone Skills, making them available for AI tools that support the Skill mechanism.

## What Are Mental Models?

Mental models are frameworks for understanding how the world works. They help us:

- Analyze problems from multiple perspectives
- Avoid cognitive biases
- Make better decisions under uncertainty
- Think more clearly and rationally

## Skills Included

Each mental model is implemented as a separate Skill. See the `.claude/skills/` directory for available models.

### General Thinking Models

These foundational thinking frameworks span multiple disciplines:

| Skill | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **Circle of Competence** | Understand your knowledge boundaries and avoid ventures into areas of ignorance. Useful for investment decisions, career development. |
| **Falsification** | A theory is scientific only if it can be proven false. Emphasize actively seeking counterexamples rather than supporting evidence. |
| **Hanlon's Razor** | "Never attribute to malice that which is adequately explained by stupidity." Avoid paranoia and analyze others' actions objectively. |
| **Inversion** | Think backwards from the outcome. Identify what would cause failure first, then work backwards to find the right course of action. |
| **Map Is Not the Territory** | Any model, theory, or description is a simplification of reality, not reality itself. Stay humble about models and metrics. |
| **Mr. Market** | A personified character created by Benjamin Graham representing market sentiment. Investors should use rather than follow market emotions. |
| **Occam's Razor** | "Entities should not be multiplied without necessity." Prefer simpler explanations with fewer assumptions. |
| **Probabilistic Thinking** | Think in terms of probabilities rather than certainties. Helps us understand risk and opportunity more clearly. |
| **Second-Order Thinking** | Consider not just direct results but also deeper indirect consequences. Every action has first-order and second/third-order effects. |
| **Thought Experiment** | Conduct hypothetical experiments in the mind to reason through problems. Useful when actual trial and error is too costly. |

### Psychology Bias Models

These models cover common cognitive biases and psychological tendencies that affect human decision-making:

| Skill | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **Trust** | The psychological mechanism that enables cooperation by believing others will act honestly. Reduces transaction costs in social interactions. |
| **Bias from Incentives** | Our judgment tilts toward self-interest when incentives are involved. "The gift blinds the wise." |
| **Pavlovian Association** | Animals (including humans) form connections between stimuli that occur simultaneously, developing subconscious likes or dislikes. |
| **Envy & Jealousy** | Painful emotions arising from comparing oneself to others who have what we desire. Can drive irrational behavior. |
| **Liking & Disliking Bias** | We tend to overestimate the merits of people/things we like and underestimate their faults, and vice versa. |
| **Denial** | When facing painful reality, humans often choose to deny or ignore it to reduce psychological discomfort. |
| **Availability Heuristic** | We judge event probability based on how easily examples come to mind. Recent or vivid events have disproportionate influence. |
| **Representativeness Heuristic** | We classify things based on similarity to a typical model, ignoring statistical base rates and other information. |
| **Social Proof** | People tend to observe others' actions to decide how to act themselves. "If everyone is doing it, it must be right." |
| **Narrative Instinct** | Humans are naturally story-telling animals. We crave narratives with causal connections to understand the world. |
| **Curiosity Instinct** | Humans have a strong desire for knowledge and are naturally interested in the unknown. Drives exploration and discovery. |
| **Language Instinct** | Humans have an innate ability to acquire grammatical language, with specialized brain mechanisms evolved for linguistic communication. |
| **First-Conclusion Bias** | The brain is like an egg—the first sperm (idea) that enters "closes" it. We tend to cling to first impressions. |
| **Overgeneralize from Small Samples** | Humans easily use small-sample experiences to derive universal laws, ignoring statistical significance. |
| **Relative Satisfaction** | Human satisfaction often depends on relative comparison rather than absolute values, leading to misreaction to opportunities. |
| **Commitment & Consistency Bias** | People tend to maintain consistency with previous commitments and behaviors, even when inappropriate. |
| **Hindsight Bias** | After an event occurs, people tend to believe they had already known or predicted the outcome. |
| **Sensitivity to Fairness** | Humans highly value fairness, and if perceiving injustice, will trigger strong emotional reactions including anger. |
| **Fundamental Attribution Error** | We tend to attribute others' behaviors to their character while underestimating situational factors. |
| **Authority Bias** | People have a tendency to obey authority directives, even when it goes against their own judgment or morality. |
| **Stress Influence** | Under high pressure, humans enter "fight or flight" mode with reduced cognitive ability, relying more on instinct and habits. |
